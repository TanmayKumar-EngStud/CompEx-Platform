These are the skills that is needed to be assessed of the user:-
1. Data Interpretation
* Skill: Understanding and extracting meaningful information from various types of graphs and charts.
* Example: Interpreting trends, patterns, and relationships in bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, scatter plots, and other graphical representations.
2. Critical Thinking
* Skill: Analyzing the data to draw logical conclusions and make informed decisions.
* Example: Determining the implications of trends, identifying correlations, and evaluating the significance of data points.
3. Quantitative Reasoning
* Skill: Applying mathematical concepts to interpret numerical data.
* Example: Calculating percentages, averages, ratios, and other statistical measures from the data presented in the graphs.
4. Attention to Detail
* Skill: Carefully observing and noting specific details in the graphical data.
* Example: Identifying exact values, recognizing subtle differences in data points, and noting any annotations or labels on the graph.
5. Synthesis of Information
* Skill: Combining information from multiple sources or different parts of the graph to form a comprehensive understanding.
* Example: Integrating data from different sections of a complex graph to answer questions that require a holistic view of the data.
6. Logical Reasoning
* Skill: Using logical reasoning to infer information that is not explicitly stated in the graph.
* Example: Making predictions based on trends, identifying potential outliers, and understanding the implications of changes in data.
7. Comparative Analysis
* Skill: Comparing data points or trends within the same graph or across different graphs.
* Example: Comparing the performance of different entities, analyzing changes over time, and identifying relative differences.
8. Pattern Recognition
* Skill: Identifying patterns and trends in the data.
* Example: Recognizing increasing or decreasing trends, identifying cyclical patterns, and understanding the relationship between variables.
9. Data Sufficiency
* Skill: Determining whether the data provided is sufficient to answer a specific question.
* Example: Assessing whether the information in the graph is adequate to make a particular inference or calculation.

## QuestionSolution

For mathematical expressions, use LaTeX with the following guidelines:
1. All LaTeX expressions MUST be enclosed between ~~ markers
2. Use standard LaTeX commands for mathematical operations:
   - Fractions: \\frac{numerator}{denominator}
   - Square roots: \\sqrt{expression}
   - Subscripts: x_{subscript}
   - Superscripts: x^{power}
   - Greek letters: \\alpha, \\beta, etc.
3. For percentages, always use \\% instead of the % symbol
4. For dollar amounts, use \\$ instead of the $ symbol
5. For text within math mode, use \\text{your text here}
6. For parentheses in math mode, use \\left( and \\right)
7. Avoid using single backslashes except for standard LaTeX commands
8. Never nest LaTeX expressions (don't put ~~ inside another ~~ block)

Example of proper LaTeX usage:
~~\\text{Rate of Change} = \\left(\\frac{\\text{Change in Y}}{\\text{Change in X}}\\right) \\text{ units per x}~~
